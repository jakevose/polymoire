Polymoire

A generative visualizer that drifts between looks and moves to whatever your computer is playing. Spotify, a browser tab, a DAW: anything you can hear.

Download {{version}} Source on GitHub

Free for Windows, macOS and Linux. Vibe coded: read that section before you install it.

Hears what you hear

It follows whatever your computer plays (kicks, tempo, loudness, mood) and records nothing. Or it plays your music from Dropbox, every beat mapped ahead.

Never the same twice

Thirteen traits (palette, field, symmetry and more) shift one or two at a time on the beat, never cutting. A new section of a song brings a new scene.

Moves with the drums

Each kick pushes the patterns forward and they coast until the next. Quiet passages move gently; drops hit harder. When the music stops, so does the picture.

Easy on the eyes

No flashing: brightness changes are small and slow. Whole-view zooms and sways are rationed to every other beat, and Reduce motion turns them off.

Five looks from the demo beat

Each picture is a real frame from the app's own shader, rendered while its built-in 120 bpm demo loop plays, just after a kick. The traits under each one are the settings that made it; in the app they drift on their own, or you can step and pin them.

Download

macOS

Open the .dmg and drag Polymoire to Applications. The app isn't signed with a paid Apple certificate, so the first launch is blocked: open System Settings → Privacy & Security, click Open Anyway next to Polymoire, then confirm. (On macOS 14 and older: right-click the app → Open.)

To hear what your Mac plays, Polymoire needs macOS 14.6 or later and asks for System Audio Recording permission once. Older versions still run the visuals and the demo beat.

Windows

Run the installer. It isn't signed with a paid certificate, so SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC": click More info → Run anyway. System audio works out of the box.

Linux

.deb: sudo apt install ./polymoire_*.deb. AppImage: chmod +x it and run it. Listens through the PulseAudio or PipeWire monitor of your default output (standard on desktop distros).

Older versions and release notes are on the releases page.

Play music from your Dropbox

The media player is optional. Dropbox only lets an app sign in with an app key, so you make a small private app in your own Dropbox account, once. It takes about five minutes, and only you can use it. If Polymoire shows Connect Dropbox with no app key box, your copy has a key built in: skip to step 2.

1. Make an app key

  1. Go to dropbox.com/developers/apps and click Create app.
  2. Choose Scoped access, then Full Dropbox. Name it anything and click Create app.
  3. On the Permissions tab, tick account_info.read, files.metadata.read, files.content.read and files.content.write, then click Submit.
  4. On the Settings tab, check that Allow public clients says Allow, and copy the App key.

2. Connect

  1. In Polymoire, click Media player (or press L).
  2. Paste the app key and click Connect Dropbox.
  3. Approve Polymoire in the browser, copy the code Dropbox shows, paste it into the app and click Finish.
  4. Pick the folder your music is in.

Keeping it safe

  • Copy the App key, never the App secret. Polymoire doesn't need the secret; don't paste it anywhere.
  • Your sign-in is kept in your system keychain (Keychain on macOS, Credential Manager on Windows, GNOME Keyring or KWallet on Linux), not in a file. Disconnect removes it.
  • files.content.write is only used for one file, Polymoire's catalog backup in your music folder.

Seeing "missing scope" or "not permitted"? Tick the missing box on the Permissions tab, click Submit, then click Disconnect in Polymoire and connect again: Dropbox only applies new permissions at sign-in. "Invalid client" usually means public clients aren't allowed, or the secret was pasted instead of the key.

Vibe coded, honestly

Polymoire was made by describing what it should do to an AI and judging the results, not by writing the code by hand. Nearly all of it (the Rust, the shaders, the tests, the build scripts and this page) was written by Claude, Anthropic's AI model, working in Claude Code. One person steered: asked for features, watched and listened to the result, and said what felt wrong.

What that means for you:

  • Nobody has read every line. The code hasn't had a human code review or a security audit.
  • It's checked by machines, not people. {{tests}} automated tests run on Windows, macOS and Linux for every change. The comfort rules (no flashing, gentle camera) are enforced by tests, and the beat detection is tested against a synthetic track with known kicks.
  • It does a small number of things. It draws to a window and reads audio. It has no telemetry or update checks. The only network access is the optional media player: once you switch to it and sign in, it talks to Dropbox and nothing else. It reads your music folder and writes exactly one file there, a backup of its catalog (a test checks the code can't call any other Dropbox write). On your computer it saves its settings and that catalog in your user app data folder, and your Dropbox sign-in in the system keychain. Every crate it depends on is listed below with a risk rating.
  • Treat it like the toy it is. If something breaks or looks off, open an issue.
